# Fuzzy Dots · Square Motion Field
## Source description, formalization, and evidence boundaries · CFD v0.2

> “The field is the square... it's almost like a motion picture inside... but it can zoom way in and way out also.”

The following preserves the creator's **first-person account**, as a source for an original interface design. It is not a diagnosis or a claim of universal visual experience.

### First-person observations as reported

- With eyes closed, body relaxed and gaze gently centered, a **square-like array** of very densely packed, fine RGB/rainbow “Fuzzy Dots” becomes noticeable.
- The dots seem able to move together, “like a drone array show,” to create shapes that reflect thoughts, mechanical ideas, and software flow diagrams.
- Sometimes a pattern appears first and is recognized later. Sometimes a consciously considered shape appears to guide it. Sometimes there is no intended subject, only geometry or flow.
- A central spiral or tornado-like formation can appear, with an impression of depth and multiple layers.
- Nested bubble / gear imagery may appear, with six-fold groupings, variable scale, toroidal forms, reversals and moving symbols.
- The field feels **square** while the motion-picture-like content inside feels navigable through apparently dramatic in/out zooms.
- The dots are perceived as **much denser** than the sparse field of CFD v0.1.

These observations are subjective and may reflect several possible visual/imagery processes; the software does **not** establish their neurological cause or external physical significance.

### Rendering principles derived from the account

1. **Fixed identity / square viewport.** The display is a bounded square window into a mathematically persistent world-coordinate field.
2. **Microdot substrate.** Dense luminous microstructure comes from a deterministic procedural lattice; current rendering samples this field rather than allocating millions of discrete particles.
3. **Motion picture.** Ambient movement is continuous even without a selected geometric form.
4. **Scale is camera, not scene.** Zoom means inspecting different scales of the same function, not creating a new unrelated random image.
5. **Behavior, not scene replacement.** Origin, drift, lattice, vortex, and six-fold nested modes act as continuous coordinate transformations on one field.
6. **Meaning is interpretive.** User-authored forms are design inspiration. A rendered gear is not evidence of underlying physical gears or access to hidden neural/model states.
7. **Provenance by default.** All current geometry and event signals are SIMULATED. Future adapters must explicitly annotate observed vs inferred signals.

### Current implementation versus future experiments

**Implemented in v0.2 PR:** square viewport, shader-sampled stable dot field, density parameter, ambient motion, eased warp-mode transitions, camera zoom, reduced-motion first-load behavior, Canvas fallback, viewport math tests.

**Not implemented:** neural imaging, gaze tracking, intent recognition, direct access to internal model reasoning, physical volumetric media, machine consciousness measurement, hardware mycelium integration.

**Known limitation:** At wide zoom and high virtual density, the dots can be much smaller than one screen pixel. GPU sampling cannot show every virtual point independently; visual aliasing and approximation are expected. Performance and actual presentation need live-device testing.

### Questions for future user review

- Are point colors more saturated, or closer to a thin rainbow phosphor haze?
- Is the boundary visibly square or only perceptually square?
- Does zoom feel like a continuous camera, or more like repeated detail at nested scales?
- Are transforms reversible without an abrupt jump?
- Does the geometry stay primarily inside a center region or occupy the whole square?
